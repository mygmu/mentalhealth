Calm Room

Student Wellbeing Companion — Policy and Standard Operating Procedure

Gulf Medical University, Student Wellbeing Center · Version 1.0 (draft for approval) · Owner: Director, Student Wellbeing Center

Status: draft for clinical approval. This document must be reviewed, amended as required, and signed off by Prof. Mayssah El Nayal before any student uses Calm Room. All thresholds, wording and contacts are provisional until approved.

1. Purpose

Calm Room is a confidential, AI-assisted first-line wellbeing companion for GMU students. It offers warm supportive listening, calming tools and short evidence-informed coping techniques, screens gently for distress, and routes students who need more help to the Student Wellbeing Center. Its purpose is early, low-barrier support and the timely identification of students who need a human counsellor.

2. Alignment with UAE and institutional frameworks

The service is designed to be consistent with the national direction for mental health in the UAE and with higher-education expectations:

  • UAE National Policy for the Promotion of Mental Health — prevention, early support, integrated care and the reduction of stigma.
  • MoHAP "Mental Health 360" — a holistic approach that treats mental and physical wellbeing as connected, emphasising prevention and early intervention.
  • Whole-institution approach to student wellbeing — early detection, stepped intervention, and the documentation of counselling activity, consistent with UAE higher-education requirements that institutions maintain approved policies and procedures for student psychological counselling.
  • GMU Student Wellbeing Center governance — Calm Room operates under the Center and its Director, and does not act independently of it.

Calm Room applies these principles through a prevention-first design (available any time, low stigma, anonymous by default), early detection (validated brief screening), stepped care (four triage levels), warm referral to a human, and secure record-keeping.

3. Scope and definitions

Calm Room provides first-line, non-clinical support. It does not diagnose, does not prescribe, and does not replace counselling, medical care, or emergency services. Screening results are indicators, not diagnoses. "The counsellor" means the Director of the Student Wellbeing Center, Prof. Mayssah El Nayal, or a delegated member of the Center.

4. Evidence base of the tools

The clinical instruments and techniques are established and openly usable:

  • Safety screen: the NIMH Ask Suicide-Screening Questions (ASQ).
  • Brief screening: PHQ-2 and GAD-2, escalating to the full PHQ-9 and GAD-7 only when indicated.
  • Coping techniques: paced breathing, 5-4-3-2-1 grounding, progressive muscle relaxation, worry-time, cognitive reframing, safe-place visualisation and the butterfly hug — all widely used in CBT, ACT and stress-management practice.
  • Supportive stance: reflective listening and open questions in the spirit of Psychological First Aid and motivational interviewing.

5. The student journey, start to finish

  1. Consent and policy. The student reads the policy and privacy notice, confirms they are 18 or older, and agrees to take part. They choose to continue anonymously or to share their details.
  2. Warm conversation. The student is welcomed and invited to talk about whatever is on their mind. The AI listens, validates, and offers practical, friendly support, and may offer a calming sound, some music, or a short technique.
  3. Continuous safety monitoring. Every message is checked for signs of crisis. Any such sign immediately moves the student to the Urgent pathway (step 7).
  4. Optional check-in. When it would help, the AI offers — never forces — a short standardised check-in (ASQ, then PHQ/GAD as indicated, and an impact question).
  5. Triage. A triage level (1–4) is calculated in code from the answers (see section 6).
  6. Guided self-help. The student is offered a calming sound or music and a guided technique, then re-rates their stress. No improvement, or a still-high score, steps the level up.
  7. Urgent pathway (if triggered). A calm crisis screen shows the helplines and emergency numbers, the counsellor is alerted automatically (section 7), and the student is encouraged to reach a person now.
  8. Referral and close. Where appropriate the student is invited to request counsellor contact. The session closes with a warm reflection, and a summary is recorded. The student may return at any time.

Thresholds are configurable in the system and must be confirmed by the counsellor. The default referral cut-offs are 10 on the PHQ-9 and GAD-7; the default severe cut-offs are 20 (PHQ-9) and 15 (GAD-7).

7. Automatic escalation to the counsellor

When a session reaches the escalation level (Level 3 or 4 by default) or any crisis is detected, the system automatically:

  1. Records an entry in the secure Referrals log with the priority (URGENT or HIGH), the triage level, the reasons, the wellbeing scores, and the student's details if shared.
  2. Sends an email alert to the Director of the Student Wellbeing Center:

The alert contains only the session summary and scores, never the conversation text. If the student chose to remain anonymous, the alert says so and provides no contact details; in that case the counsellor follows the Center's safeguarding procedure for anonymous disclosures. The counsellor, or a delegate, reviews the Referrals queue on each working day and updates each case status (New, Contacted, Appointment booked, Closed, Unreachable).

Out-of-hours limitation. Calm Room is not monitored in real time, and email alerts are actioned during working hours. Urgent cases are always directed, in the moment, to 999, 998, the nearest hospital, and the national line 800 4673 (800 HOPE), which operates daily 8am–8pm.

8. Crisis and safeguarding procedure

  1. The app shows the crisis screen with the national and emergency contacts and encourages the student to reach a person immediately.
  2. An URGENT alert is sent to the counsellor and logged.
  3. The counsellor, on receipt, follows the Student Wellbeing Center's safeguarding and risk-management procedure, including any duty to involve GMU health services or emergency services where a student is identifiable and at imminent risk.
  4. All actions are documented in the Center's records.

9. Data protection, privacy and records

  • Minimal data. Calm Room stores only a session summary and wellbeing scores. The conversation text is never stored.
  • Anonymity by default. Students may use the service without giving any identifying information. Names and contact details are stored only when the student chooses to share them, or submits a referral request.
  • On-device personal tools. The mood tracker, journal, personal plan and safety plan are stored only in the student's own browser. The university holds no copy, and the student can delete them at any time.
  • Access control. Session summaries, referrals and the counsellor dashboard are restricted to authorised Student Wellbeing Center staff, protected by a private passcode and, where enabled, named Google-account access.
  • AI processing. Messages are processed by a third-party AI service to generate replies. The provider and its data-handling terms must be approved by GMU before launch, and the privacy notice must name the arrangement chosen.
  • Retention and rights. Records are retained and disposed of in line with GMU policy. Students may ask the Center what is held about them. The service follows UAE data-protection principles.

10. Student policy and consent notice (shown in the app)

The following plain-language notice is presented on the welcome screen, and the student must agree before continuing. It is editable by the Center.

What Calm Room is. A confidential, AI-assisted first-line wellbeing companion from the GMU Student Wellbeing Center. It offers supportive listening, calming tools and coping techniques, in line with the UAE National Policy for the Promotion of Mental Health. It is not a therapist, cannot diagnose, and does not replace professional care.

Not for emergencies. It is not monitored in real time. If you are in danger or thinking of harming yourself, use Urgent help, call 999 or 998, or go to the nearest hospital.

Your choice to stay anonymous. You may use Calm Room without giving your name. If you share your details, they are used only so the Center can follow up with you.

How your information is used. Your messages are processed by an AI service to generate replies. Calm Room keeps only a short summary and your wellbeing scores, never the conversation. Summaries and any contact details are visible only to authorised Center staff.

What stays on your device. Your daily mood check-ins, journal, personal plan and safety plan are stored only in your own browser on your own device. They are never sent to the university. A journal entry is sent to the AI service only if you tap Reflect with me. On a shared device, please delete your data when you finish.

When a counsellor may be alerted. If your answers suggest you may be at risk, or if you ask to be contacted, an alert with your scores and summary (and your contact details only if you shared them) is sent to Prof. Mayssah El Nayal and recorded securely, to keep you safe.

Your rights. Taking part is voluntary and you can stop at any time. You may ask the Center what is held about you. This service follows GMU policy and UAE data-protection principles.

By continuing, you confirm that you have read and understood this policy and agree to take part.

11. Clinical content governance

The AI persona, the screening thresholds, the triage rules, the exercises and all student-facing wording are stored as editable settings and are owned by the Student Wellbeing Center. No change to clinical content is made without the counsellor's approval. The AI is instructed never to diagnose, never to advise on medication, and never to claim to be human.

12. Limitations

  • Calm Room provides first-line support only and can make mistakes; it is not a substitute for assessment by a qualified professional.
  • Screening tools indicate possible distress; they do not establish a diagnosis.
  • Automated crisis detection is a safety net and may not catch every case; it does not replace clinical judgement.
  • The service is not for emergencies and is not monitored in real time.

13. Review and approval

This policy and procedure is reviewed at least annually, or sooner if the service, the instruments, or GMU or UAE policy change. The Student Wellbeing Center records the outcome of each review.
